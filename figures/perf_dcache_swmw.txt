DLM+SW dentry cache -- transition-chain serialization strategies
================================================================
2x96 EPYC 9654, one hw thread/core (0..191), jemalloc, best-of-5, conservation-
gated.  dirs decontended to 16/writer (so the ONLY writer contention is the
transition chain, not the index buckets).  data: scripts/dcache_swmw.csv;
figure: figures/dcache_swmw.png; sweep: scripts/run_dcache_swmw.sh.

The transition chain is a single-producer / multi-consumer list: the demote
(ENQUEUE) has one producer under the bucket lock; the folds (DEQUEUE) are many
consumers.  Four arms -- identical reader + mark; only the chain differs:
  dlm-chainlock  -DDC_CHAIN_LOCK: legacy per-host CHAIN LOCK -- demote AND folds
                 take it.  176 B.  The reference baseline.
  dlm-swmw       -DDC_CHAIN_SWMW: SW enqueue + MW dequeue -- the chain rides the
                 mixed commit as MW records (lock-free folds).  168 B.
  dlm-swmw-pad   dlm-swmw + 8 B dead padding -> 176 B, the SAME-SIZE control.
  dlm-foldlock   DEFAULT (no chain flag): SW enqueue + per-host FOLD LOCK dequeue,
                 plain chain stores, no MW descriptor.  The producer does NOT take
                 the fold lock -- only fold workers do.  176 B.

CONCLUSIONS
-----------
1. FOLD LOCK is the best of the three, and is now the DEFAULT.  It nearly matches
   the chain lock uncontended (0.89x at 1 thread, vs 0.61x for the MW dequeue) and
   beats BOTH alternatives at every higher thread count -- 1.3-1.8x over the MW
   dequeue and 2.7-5.5x over the chain lock from 8 to 192 threads.  Why: the plain
   chain stores under a rarely-contended per-host lock avoid the MW dequeue's
   descriptor + call_rcu per fold (recovering the uncontended cost), while
   decoupling the producer from that lock avoids the chain lock's producer-vs-fold
   coupling (recovering the scaling).

2. The MW dequeue (dlm-swmw) is a TRADE-OFF: lock-free folds scale, but the
   descriptor cost makes it ~1.6x slower than the chain lock uncontended (1 thread).
   Its win over the chain lock is the lock-free chain, NOT the -8B: dlm-swmw (168 B)
   and dlm-swmw-pad (176 B) track each other within noise across every panel.

3. The chain lock (reference) plateaus with concurrency (its demote couples the
   producer with the folds on one per-host word) and even DEGRADES as the rename
   fraction rises.

4. Reads are a wash when uncontended (all four ~1290 Mlk/s -- identical reader).
   Under heavy concurrent rename load the mixed arms' readers run somewhat slower
   than the chain-lock readers (the mixed commit's call_rcu/reclaim traffic on the
   shared machine) -- but the mixed writers are doing far more renames.
